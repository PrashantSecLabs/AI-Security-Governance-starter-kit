## 1. Purpose

This policy sets out how [Company Name] ("the Company") may use artificial intelligence (AI) tools so that we gain the benefits while protecting our clients, our people, our data and our reputation.

## 2. Scope

This policy applies to all employees, contractors, consultants and temporary staff ("users") who use AI tools for Company work, on any device, including personal devices and personal accounts when used for Company information.

It covers generative AI chat assistants, AI features embedded in other software (for example email, office suites, meeting tools, code editors and CRM), AI agents, and AI services accessed through an API.

## 3. Definitions

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| AI tool | Any software or service that uses machine learning or generative AI to produce text, code, images, audio, video, decisions or recommendations |
| Approved tool | An AI tool listed on the Company's approved AI tool register (section 6) |
| Shadow AI | Use of an AI tool that has not been approved, including free or personal accounts used for work |
| AI agent | An AI tool that can take actions (send email, change files, run code, call other systems) rather than only produce content |
| Company data | Any information the Company holds or processes, including client, employee, financial and technical information |

## 4. Principles

1. **Humans stay accountable.** AI assists; the user is responsible for the work they submit.
2. **Protect data first.** Only the right data goes into the right tool.
3. **Verify before you rely.** AI output can be wrong, biased or out of date.
4. **Be transparent.** Disclose AI use where clients, law or Company rules require it.
5. **Use approved tools.** Do not work around controls.

## 5. Roles and responsibilities

| Role | Responsibility |
| --- | --- |
| Policy owner | Maintains this policy, the approved tool register and the AI risk register; reports AI risks to leadership |
| Managers | Make sure their teams know and follow the policy; approve use cases within their area |
| IT / Security | Assess and configure tools, manage access, monitor for misuse, support incident response |
| Legal / Data protection contact | Advises on data protection, contracts, intellectual property and regulatory obligations |
| All users | Follow this policy, complete AI training, and report concerns and incidents promptly |

> "In a small business one person may hold several of these roles. That is fine, as long as someone is named for each."

## 6. Approved tools and requesting new ones

6.1 Only approved tools may be used with Company data. The current list is maintained at [location of register].

6.2 To request a new AI tool or a new use of an approved tool, submit [request form / email to X] with the business purpose, the data involved and the vendor. IT/Security will review it using the AI Tool and Vendor Review Checklist and respond within [X working days].

6.3 AI features that are switched on by default inside existing software are treated as new tools until reviewed.

## 7. Data rules

Map these rows to your own data classification labels.

| Data type | Examples | Approved tools | Unapproved or personal tools |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Public** | Published marketing content, public web pages | Allowed | Allowed |
| **Internal** | Internal procedures, non-sensitive meeting notes | Allowed | Not allowed |
| **Confidential** | Client work product, contracts, pricing, source code, unreleased plans | Allowed only where the tool's terms prohibit training on our data and access is by company login (SSO/MFA) | Never |
| **Restricted** | Personal data of individuals, health or financial records, credentials, secrets, privileged legal material, regulated data | Only with written approval from [owner] for a specific use case | Never |

7.1 Never enter passwords, API keys, private keys, authentication tokens or recovery codes into an AI tool.

7.2 Where practical, remove or anonymise names and identifiers before using Confidential data.

7.3 Client data may be used with AI only where the client contract allows it. If unsure, ask [owner] first.

## 8. Acceptable uses

Within the data rules above, approved tools may be used to:

- draft, summarise, translate and edit text
- brainstorm ideas, outlines and options
- analyse non-sensitive data and explain technical concepts
- write, review and explain code, with the checks in section 10
- automate repetitive, low-risk tasks

## 9. Prohibited uses

Users must not use AI tools to:

- process Confidential or Restricted data in unapproved or personal tools
- make or finalise decisions about people (hiring, firing, promotion, pay, discipline, credit or similar) without meaningful human review
- generate content that is deceptive, discriminatory, harassing, unlawful or that infringes others' rights
- impersonate a real person, or create synthetic voice, image or video of a real person without their consent
- attempt to bypass security controls, extract other users' data or test a tool's defences without authorisation
- present AI output as verified fact, legal advice, medical advice or professional opinion without qualified human review
- install AI browser extensions, plugins, connectors or agents that access Company accounts or data without approval

## 10. Human oversight and verification

10.1 Review all AI output for accuracy, completeness, bias and suitability before using it.

10.2 Check facts, figures, quotations, citations and legal or regulatory statements against reliable sources. AI tools can produce confident but false content.

10.3 Test and review AI-generated code, scripts, queries and configurations as you would any code from an unknown source, including security review, before deploying.

10.4 Do not allow AI agents to take irreversible or high-impact actions (sending external communications, deleting data, moving money, changing access rights or production systems) without human approval.

## 11. Clients, third parties and transparency

11.1 Follow client contract terms on AI use and disclosure.

11.2 Tell clients, customers or the public when AI was used where required by law, contract or Company rules, and always when they are interacting directly with an AI system.

11.3 Do not upload third-party confidential information (including information shared under NDA) to AI tools unless the owner has agreed.

## 12. Intellectual property and confidentiality

12.1 Check the tool's terms on ownership and use of outputs before using output in client deliverables or products.

12.2 Do not enter material you do not have the right to share. Do not use AI to copy or closely imitate others' copyrighted work.

12.3 Treat AI chat histories and prompts as Company records; they may be retained, reviewed and produced in an investigation or legal process.

## 13. Security requirements for AI tools

13.1 Access AI tools using Company-managed accounts with single sign-on and multi-factor authentication where available. No shared accounts.

13.2 Keep AI-related integrations to a minimum. Connectors, plugins and agents must have the least access needed and be approved before use.

13.3 Report lost or compromised accounts that have access to AI tools immediately.

13.4 Treat content from emails, web pages and documents as untrusted when an AI tool processes it. Hidden instructions in that content can manipulate the tool (prompt injection).

## 14. Training and AI literacy

All users must complete AI awareness training on joining and at least every [12] months. It covers this policy, data rules, verification of outputs and how to report incidents. Where applicable law requires measures to support AI literacy, this training forms part of the Company's response.

## 15. Reporting incidents and concerns

Report immediately to [contact / mailbox] if you:

- entered Confidential or Restricted data into a tool that is not approved
- suspect an AI tool has leaked, exposed or misused data
- notice harmful, biased or unlawful AI output affecting a client, colleague or decision
- receive an AI-generated message, voice or video you suspect is impersonating someone
- see an AI agent act outside what it was asked to do

Reports made in good faith will not lead to disciplinary action solely for reporting. Incidents are handled under the Company's incident response procedure.

## 16. Exceptions

Exceptions need written approval from [owner], with the business reason, the risk and compensating controls recorded, and a review date no more than [6] months ahead.

## 17. Enforcement

Breaches of this policy may lead to disciplinary action, up to and including termination, and, for contractors, termination of the engagement. Serious breaches may be reported to clients, regulators or law enforcement where required.

## Appendix A: One-page quick reference for staff

**Do**
- Use approved tools, signed in with your company account
- Remove names and identifiers where you can
- Check facts, numbers and sources yourself
- Test and review any AI-written code
- Tell someone if something goes wrong

**Don't**
- Paste passwords, keys, client secrets or personal data into AI tools
- Use free or personal AI accounts for work
- Let an AI send, delete or approve anything on its own
- Trust AI output without checking it
- Install AI plugins or extensions without approval

**Unsure? Ask [contact] before you paste.**

## Adaptation notes (delete before issuing)

- **Regional rules.** If you operate in or serve the EU, check the EU AI Act duties that apply to you as a deployer, including AI literacy and transparency, as well as GDPR. In the US there is no single federal AI law, and state and sector rules vary. Ask your legal adviser.
- **Contracts.** Check client contracts and insurance policies for AI-related clauses.
- **HR.** If AI use will be monitored, check employee-monitoring and consultation rules in your country before switching monitoring on.
- **Keep it short.** A policy people read beats a perfect one they ignore. Cut sections that do not apply.
