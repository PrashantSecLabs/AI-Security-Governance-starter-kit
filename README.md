# AI Security and Governance Starter Kit

A free, practical starter kit for **small businesses and independent consultants** who are using AI tools and need simple governance without a big compliance programme.

**Created by [Prashant Pahwa](https://www.linkedin.com/in/prashant-pahwa-cism/), CISM** | Security operations and governance practitioner

## What's inside

| File | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| [01-AI-Acceptable-Use-Policy.md](01-AI-Acceptable-Use-Policy.md) | Editable policy template: data rules by classification, prohibited uses, human oversight, agent and plugin rules, incident reporting, and a one-page staff quick reference |
| [02-AI-Risk-Register.xlsx](02-AI-Risk-Register.xlsx) | Risk register with 21 starter risks, automatic scoring and ratings, a summary sheet and a scoring guide. A plain [CSV copy](02-AI-Risk-Register.csv) of the starter risks is included for viewing on GitHub |
| [03-AI-Tool-and-Vendor-Review-Checklist.md](03-AI-Tool-and-Vendor-Review-Checklist.md) | 5-minute triage plus a detailed checklist for reviewing AI tools and vendors, with a decision record and vendor questions to copy and paste |

## How to use it

1. **Start with the policy.** Replace the `[bracketed]` items, delete the notes, and have your legal or HR adviser review it before you issue it.
2. **Make a list of AI tools in use**, including AI features switched on inside software you already pay for.
3. **Review each tool** with the checklist. Approve it, approve it with conditions, or reject it.
4. **Open the risk register.** Re-score the starter risks for your business, delete what does not apply, and assign owners.
5. **Review quarterly**, and after any AI-related incident or major tool change.

## How it lines up with common references

The kit is written to be useful on its own. The references below are for orientation, and using the kit does not make you conformant with or certified against any of them.

- **OWASP Top 10 for LLM Applications (2026 edition)**: the register tags each risk with the matching entry.
- **NIST AI Risk Management Framework 1.0 (AI 100-1)**: risks are tagged with the Govern, Map, Measure or Manage function.
- **NIST AI 600-1 (Generative AI Profile, July 2024)**: risks are tagged with its generative AI risk areas.
- **ISO/IEC 42001 (AI management systems)**: if you later want a formal management system, the policy, register and review records are a reasonable starting point.

## Regulatory snapshot (check before relying on it)

This was accurate when the kit was prepared in November 2026. Rules change, and this is not legal advice.

- **EU AI Act:** the Digital Omnibus on AI (Regulation (EU) 2026/1744) entered into force on 27 July 2026. It reworded the AI literacy duty in Article 4 so that providers and deployers must take measures to support AI literacy. Transparency duties under Article 50 are still scheduled from 2 August 2026, with a grace period to 2 December 2026 for machine-readable marking of content from systems already on the market. High-risk obligations for stand-alone (Annex III) systems were deferred to 2 December 2027 and for products covered by Annex I to 2 August 2028.
- **United States:** there is no single federal AI law. State and sector rules vary, so check the ones that apply to you and to your clients.
- **NIST:** AI RMF 1.0 is being revised. Check nist.gov for the current version.
- **OWASP:** the LLM Top 10 was updated in August 2026. Check genai.owasp.org for the current list.

## Limits

- These are **templates and starting points**, not legal, compliance or audit advice, and no one should treat them as a certification of anything.
- The starter risks, scores and controls are examples to prompt discussion. They are not an assessment of your business.
- The kit has not yet been tested in a live workshop or audit. Feedback is welcome.
- It covers practical use of third-party AI tools. It does not cover building or training your own models.
